Tahoe Timber Planner

Tahoe's forests are two to four times denser than they should be, and wood from thinning them is trucked 70 to 100 miles away. Tahoe Wood turns that wood into nail-laminated timber (NLT) panels made here. Sketch a backyard home or ADU from those panels. The planner checks what a Tahoe lot allows, sizes the roof for snow, and lists every panel with a price guide and the carbon stored in the wood. Everything snaps to a 4-ft plywood module, so the same grid carries over to local CLT.

    Ridge beam and posts
    Logs and NLT beams use local wood but span less than glulam, so heavy snow means more posts. The planner adds posts on a 2-ft half-panel grid until the beam works.
    Ridge support
    A center bearing wall carries the roof along the beam line with no posts, and a short header spans its doorway. It splits the home lengthwise, for example bedroom and bath on one side and living on the other. Tied rafters skip the ridge beam entirely. The two roof slopes lean on each other at the peak, and steel ties across the room at wall-top height keep the walls from spreading. They work on gable roofs only.

      Utilities
      All-electric with heat pumps, so there's no gas line or meter. Septic isn't an option in the Tahoe Basin. State law requires sewage to be piped out of the basin, so every home connects to sewer, and most parcels have public water rather than wells. The planner sites the home near the house to keep the utility trench short. Each water and sewer district decides whether a detached ADU may share hookups, and California law requires any ADU connection fee to be proportionate to the unit's size.
        Ways to lower the cost
        • Stay under 750 ft². California bars impact fees on ADUs that size.
        • Keep it a simple rectangle on the 4-ft grid. Panels arrive cut to size, with little waste and a short crane day.
        • Leave the NLT ceiling exposed. The structure becomes the finished wood ceiling, so there's no drywall or paint overhead.
        • Use a pre-approved standard plan. It cuts architect, engineering and plan-check time for every home that repeats it.
        • Tie into the main house's sewer, water and electric, and site the ADU close to the house. The trench stays short and there are no new meters.
        • Go all-electric with heat pumps. There's no gas line, meter or trench.
        • Build two homes at once. They share site work, utility runs and mobilization.
        • Take the weathertight shell and finish the inside yourself, or with a local finish carpenter.
        • Deed-restrict it as workforce housing. That can open fee waivers, bonus units and housing grants where they're offered.

          Price and engineering assumptions

          NLT pricing is an early estimate from local partners. "Rest of build" covers walls, windows, roofing, insulation, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and finishes. The range shown is −15% to +20% around the midpoint.

          Panel depths are a theoretical screening calculation on the plywood-size module, to show the method. They are not an engineering design. The calculation assumes:

          • Hem-Fir No. 2 lumber with NDS size factors and a 1.15 snow load-duration factor.
          • A 0.8 reduction on strength and stiffness for butt joints.
          • ASCE 7-22 flat-roof snow pf = 0.7·Ce·Ct·pg with no slope reduction. Ce = 1.0, or 1.2 when the lot is sheltered among conifers. Ct = 1.2, the upper end of ASCE 7-22 Table 7.3-3 for a heated, insulated, unventilated roof. Loads are applied at 0.7 for allowable-stress design, because ASCE 7-22 ground snow values are strength-level.
          • Gable roofs at 7:12 or flatter are also checked for ASCE 7-22 §7.6.1 unbalanced snow: the full ground snow load on the leeward side, with the windward side unloaded. This is applied to every gable layout as a conservative simplification.
          • Snow deflection increased 1.5× for creep, limited to span/240.
          • A floor load of 40 psf, limited to span/480, with spans capped at 16 ft.

          When the roof needs a ridge beam or beam lines, the planner sizes a 24F-V4 glulam (Fb 2,400 psi, E 1.8 million psi) for bending, shear and span/240. Round logs use placeholder values for visually graded local pine or fir (Fb 1,000 psi, E 1.1 million psi) until logs are graded. NLT beams are 3 to 8 plies of 2x12 Hem-Fir No. 2 nailed together. CLT beams are a preview. They use a 5-ply, 6⅞-in panel on edge with generic PRG 320 E1 values (Fb 1,950 psi with a 0.85 reduction, E 1.7 million psi) until Alpenglow's panels are certified. Tied rafters treat the gable as a three-hinged frame. A36 steel rods at wall-top height (allowable 0.33 × 58 ksi on the gross area) take the outward push, H = w·W²/(8·rise), under balanced snow. It adds evenly spaced interior posts on a 2-ft half-panel grid, each with its own pier or footing, when a single span won't work. The walls are load-bearing. They carry the roof panels and the beam ends, but wall capacity, headers over openings, earthquake and wind bracing, and connections aren't checked. A licensed engineer will replace all of this with stamped span tables.
