    How lumber is counted: lumber cost = board feet in the whole panel × price per board foot × (1 + waste). A 2×10 ft rough-sawn 2x6 panel holds 12 boards × 10 ft = 120 board feet, so it's about $126 of lumber at $1. An earlier draft of the financials counted one board (10 board feet) instead of the whole panel, which understated cost by about $115 per panel.

    What NLT is

    Nail-laminated timber is an engineered wood panel made by standing dimensional lumber on edge and nailing the boards together. Panels work as floors, roofs, walls and ceilings, and NLT is recognized by the International Building Code. It suits Tahoe-area softwoods like pine and fir, including wood from forest thinning, and it needs very little equipment to start.

    What you need

    • A jig to hold boards square. A simple one can be built from 2x4s and plywood.
    • A pneumatic nailer, compressor and clamps.
    • Dry, straight lumber, with a moisture meter to check it.
    • Covered space to build and store panels, plus a plan for transport by panel size and weight.

    Fasteners and durability

    For exposed NLT, use stainless steel or hot-dip galvanized fasteners for long-term durability. Check the building code's decay-protection requirements for wood exposed to weather or near the ground. Panels used in structures need an engineer's design. See Think Wood's NLT Design and Construction Guide.

    Assumptions behind the math
    • Boards per panel = panel width ÷ board thickness. Rough-sawn boards are a full 2 in thick; surfaced boards are 1½ in.
    • Board feet per foot of board = thickness × depth ÷ 12. Rough-sawn uses full nominal sizes (a 2x6 is 2 × 6 in); surfaced uses actual sizes (1½ × 5½ in).
    • Nails per panel = boards × (panel length ÷ nail spacing) × nails per spot.
    • Labor hours per panel = panel area ÷ throughput. Weight is estimated at 2.5 lb per board foot.
    • Breakeven panels = (equipment + working capital) ÷ margin per panel, before your own wages beyond the labor line.
